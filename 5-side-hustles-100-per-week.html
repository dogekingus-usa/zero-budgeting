5 Side Hustles That Pay $100+ Per Week

Published: May 21, 2026 | Reading time: 6 min

Let's talk about real numbers. Not "make money while you sleep" hype. Not "passive income with zero work." Real side hustles that put $100+ in your pocket every single week.

That's $400+ per month  enough to cover a car payment, build an emergency fund, or finally start investing. And you don't need a degree, a following, or startup capital to get there.

I've tested and researched dozens of side hustles to find the ones that consistently hit the $100/week mark for beginners. These five are the real deal in 2026.

#1: Freelance Writing  $100$500/Week

Before you say "I'm not a writer"  hear me out. Freelance writing doesn't mean writing novels or poetry. It means writing blog posts, product descriptions, email newsletters, and social media captions for businesses that don't have time to do it themselves.

Businesses are desperate for content in 2026. AI tools can generate drafts, but they still need real humans to add personality, fact-check, and tailor the tone. That's where you come in.

How to start this week:

  • Create a profile on Upwork and Fiverr  Offer blog writing at $0.05$0.10 per word. A 1,000-word article at $0.10 = $100. Two articles = $200.
  • Pitch 10 local businesses  Small businesses near you need website copy, email blasts, and blog content. Offer to write one sample article free to prove yourself.
  • Join niche writing platforms  Scripted, Contently, and iWriter all connect beginners with paying clients.

Real talk: Your first week might only land one or two gigs. But as you build reviews and samples, $100/week becomes your floor, not your ceiling.

#2: Virtual Assistant  $100$400/Week

Every busy entrepreneur, solopreneur, and small business owner needs help with the stuff they hate doing: scheduling, email management, data entry, customer service, and social media posting. That's where you come in as a Virtual Assistant (VA).

The beauty of VA work? It pays consistently. You're not chasing one-off gigs  you're landing weekly retainers that guarantee your $100+ per week.

How to start this week:

  • Define your 3 core services  Example: "I manage your inbox, schedule your social posts, and handle customer emails." Keep it simple.
  • Create a one-page service sheet  List what you do, your rate ($15$25/hour to start), and a testimonial slot (leave it blank until you get your first client).
  • Post on LinkedIn and Facebook VA groups  "Hi, I'm offering virtual assistant services starting at $15/hour. I specialize in email management and scheduling. DM me if you need an extra pair of hands."
  • Check Belay, Time Etc, and Zirtual  These agencies hire beginners and match you with clients.

Pro tip: Offer a "first week trial" at 50% off. Once they see how much time you save them, they'll happily pay full price.

#3: Online Tutoring  $100$600/Week

If you're proficient in any subject  English, math, science, music, or even a specific software tool  you can tutor online and easily clear $100/week. Platforms like Preply, Cambly, and Chegg connect tutors with students worldwide.

English tutoring alone pays $10$30/hour depending on your experience and the platform. Tutor 10 hours at $15/hour and you've hit your $100 target by Wednesday.

How to start this week:

  • Sign up on Preply or Cambly  Both accept beginners. Create a profile highlighting your teaching style and availability.
  • Price slightly below market to get your first students  Once you have 510 reviews, raise your rate.
  • Set a schedule and stick to it  2 hours per evening = 10 hours per week = easy $100$200.

#4: Food Delivery / Rideshare  $100$700/Week

DoorDash, Uber Eats, Uber, and Lyft are the most accessible side hustles on this list. You can start earning within hours of signing up. No resume, no interview, no waiting for approvals.

In most mid-sized US cities, driving 1015 hours per week during peak dinner hours (5 PM9 PM) and weekend lunch easily clears $100$300 per week after expenses.

Maximize your earnings:

  • Drive during peak hours only  Dinner rush (59 PM) and weekend lunch (11 AM2 PM) pay 23x more than off-peak.
  • Use a fuel rewards credit card  Save 510% on gas, which directly boosts your per-hour earnings.
  • Track your mileage for tax deductions  The IRS mileage deduction for 2026 is $0.67/mile, which can significantly reduce your taxable income.

Warning: Factor in gas, maintenance, and wear-and-tear. Your gross pay might be $200, but your net after costs is closer to $100$150. Still solid  just know the real numbers.

This is the holy grail of side hustles for one simple reason: you create it once and sell it forever. Digital products  templates, planners, guides, courses, and toolkits  require no inventory, no shipping, and no ongoing time investment per sale.
