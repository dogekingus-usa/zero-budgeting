Your credit report contains information about your financial history, including payment patterns, debt levels, and the types of accounts you have.   • Credit reports contain your financial history.
  • Credit scores are calculated based on the data in these reports.
  • A higher score indicates better creditworthiness.

Add an Authorized User to Your Credit Card

If you have a family member or friend with good credit, adding them as an authorized user on your card can be a powerful way to build their and your credit. This method allows the authorized user—(TM)s name to appear on your credit report, potentially improving both of your scores.

Steps:

  • Contact your bank or credit card issuer to request adding an authorized user.
  • Choose someone with a strong financial track record.
  • Maintain regular communication about account usage and payments.

Sign Up for Utility Services in Your Name

Many utility companies report your payment history to credit bureaus, so signing up for services like electricity, gas, or internet can positively impact your credit score. Ensure you pay these bills on time and in full each month.

Tips:

  • Choose reputable service providers that report to major credit bureaus.
  • Set up automatic payments to avoid late fees or disconnections.
  • Check your utility bills and payment history regularly for accuracy.

Utilize Public Libraries

Many libraries offer free services that can help you build credit. For example, some public libraries provide access to financial education resources and even allow users to report their library payments as a form of payment history. Check with your local library for available programs.

Library Programs:

  • Financial workshops that teach credit management skills.
  • Payment reporting services through partnerships with financial institutions.
