How to Start Investing with Less Than $100

Published: May 14, 2026 | Reading time: 4 min

Welcome to Your First Steps in Investing

Starting your journey into the world of investments can feel overwhelming, especially when you don't have much money to begin with. But fear not! This guide will walk you through how to start investing with as little as $100. Whether you're saving for retirement or looking to grow your savings, these steps are designed to be simple and effective.

Why Start Investing?

Investing is a powerful tool that can help you build wealth over time. Even small investments can grow significantly with the power of compound interest. By starting early, even with a limited budget, you're setting yourself up for financial security in the future.

Step 1: Understand Your Financial Goals

Before diving into any investment, it's crucial to define your goals. Ask yourself:

  • What do I want to achieve with my investments?
  • Do I need a short-term or long-term investment strategy?
  • How much risk am I willing to take?

Step 2: Choose Your Investment Options

With your goals in mind, here are some practical investment options that require minimal initial capital:

  • Stocks: Look for micro-cap or fractional shares. Many platforms allow you to invest as little as $5.
  • Bonds: Consider government bonds or bond ETFs, which can be purchased with small amounts through online brokers.
  • Mutual Funds: These are pools of money managed by professionals. Some mutual funds require a minimum investment as low as $100.
  • Cryptocurrencies: Platforms like Coinbase allow you to buy cryptocurrencies with small amounts, often starting at $10 or less per transaction.

Step 3: Open an Investment Account

Once you've chosen your investment, it's time to open an account. Many online brokerages offer low-cost and beginner-friendly options:

  • Robinhood: Known for its commission-free trading and user-friendly interface.
  • Acorns: Great for investing small amounts of money with a focus on dollar-cost averaging.
  • Stash: Offers low minimum investments, including fractional shares in popular companies.

Step 4: Start Small and Be Patient

When you're just starting out, it's best to begin with small investments. Here are some tips to help you get started:

  • Dollar-Cost Averaging (DCA): Invest a fixed amount of money at regular intervals. This strategy can help reduce the impact of market volatility.
  • Regular Contributions: Automate your investments to ensure you're consistently adding to your portfolio over time.
  • Stay Informed: Keep an eye on your investments but avoid making impulsive decisions based on short-term market fluctuations.

Step 5: Monitor and Adjust Your Investments

While it's important to be patient, regular monitoring can help you make informed adjustments. Here are a few steps:

  • Evaluate Performance: Review your investments periodically to ensure they align with your financial goals.
  • Tax Efficiency: Be aware of tax implications and seek ways to minimize them, such as investing in tax-advantaged accounts like IRAs or 401(k)s if applicable.
  • Diversification: Consider spreading your investments across different asset classes to reduce risk.

Conclusion: Embrace Your Journey into Investing

Starting with less than $100 is a great way to dip your toes into the world of investing. Remember, every little bit helps, and the key to success lies in consistency and patience. As you grow more comfortable, consider increasing your investment amounts and exploring more advanced strategies.
